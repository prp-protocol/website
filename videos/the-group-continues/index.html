The Group Continues

A fictional international assembly illustrates how PRP groupcast organizes participation while independent multicast streams carry delegates' voices to one another and to a wider public audience.

English narration and burned-in English captions. Portuguese text is a translation, not dubbing. Visual descriptions are text, not an audio-description track. Optional subtitles do not replace the English captions embedded in the image.

Complete narration

0:00 · Many voices, a wider audience

An assembly brings together delegations from many places. Hundreds of people can take part in the discussion. Millions may want to follow it. How can this meeting organize participation and distribute its voices without concentrating all media in one application server?

0:19 · Participation has its own reference

In PRP, groupcast gives the group its own reference and participation governed by authorized identities. The assembly application decides who may take the floor. Group membership does not automatically grant every right to speak or transmit.

0:37 · Independent sources

Amara presents her proposal. Kenji responds. Each stream has its own reference and authorized source. The application associates these streams with the meeting. The group organizes participants without turning all their voices into a single source.

0:56 · Shared distribution paths

Aggregators distribute each stream along paths to its recipients. Shared stretches can feed further branches. The source does not need to send a separate copy to every viewer. Distribution grows through cooperation across the network.

1:13 · Watching and deliberating

The assembly chooses which streams to make public. Millions can follow without joining the deliberating group. Carrying these voices does not grant the right to admit delegates, claim authorship, or control who may speak.

1:28 · The group continues

One stream ends. Another begins. Sessions can change while the group continues. This organization depends on valid state, available paths, and capacity.

1:41 · Return to the audience

Many voices take part. A larger audience follows. The group organizes participation. The network distributes the streams. Groupcast. PRP.
