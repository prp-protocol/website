Let each operator enforce local access and resource priorities

Each transport scope can administer its resources and establish and enforce local access and priority conditions, respecting agreements with participants, without requiring one uniform policy throughout the network. Identify the scope, policy owner, controlled resources and enforcement point. Scopes may be adjacent or nested; recursive layers and hidden internal topology are not required. Configurable local preferences alone do not suffice without control of the relevant resources. F36 separately assesses participant-requested service quality, and F37 response to congestion. Local autonomy does not imply unlimited resources, arbitrary violation of agreements or absence of higher-level constraints.

Rationale

batadv_send_skb_packet constructs ETH_P_BATMAN Ethernet frames and calls dev_queue_xmit on the hard interface, explicitly noting possible drops from congestion or traffic shaping. Policy belongs on this physical egress rather than only on the virtual batman interface. The selected Linux implementation sends native frames through dev_queue_xmit. The core selects the outgoing device queue and invokes its configured qdisc. An operator can attach PRIO to this resource: classification selects priority bands and dequeue serves higher bands first. The u32 classifier matches masked bytes at configured offsets without requiring an IP header; classifier actions can refuse local egress. TBF children gate transmission on tokens and bound queued bytes through their default inner queues. This supplies an enforcement point for local admission, priority and rate policy, independently at each participating Linux node, without a uniform network-wide policy.

Assessment method

Native transmit-path and optional local qdisc source audit

Source context

Linux v6.12 native transmit and traffic-control sources

Outstanding qualification

Optional Linux deployment composition, explicitly selected for F38; requires administrator setup and the relevant scheduler/classifier modules. This source audit establishes local outgoing-frame resource enforcement, not protocol-native negotiation or a tested configuration. Classifier offsets must match the selected native frame layout. Rate accounting is at the netdevice queue, not exact radio airtime; previously consumed ingress capacity is not recovered. No authenticated per-user entitlement, distributed reservation, end-to-end QoS, universal hardware support or default-policy claim. Configure each TBF with its default bounded inner queue; replacing that child can invalidate its queue-limit assumption. No runtime experiment or other-cell promotion.
